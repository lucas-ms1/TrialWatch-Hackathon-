### Presumption of innocence — 2

Standard: Everyone charged with a criminal offence is presumed innocent until proved guilty according to law; the prosecution bears the burden and authorities must refrain from prejudging guilt.

Stage: Criminal charge through adjudication.

Exceptions/applicability: Prosecution, detention or an adverse verdict alone does not establish failure; distinguish official statements and presentation from a properly reached finding.

Observable components: Burden and proof standard; Benefit of doubt; Official prejudgment; Presentation of accused

Factual questions: Who bore the burden and what proof standard was used?; Did authorities prejudge guilt or present the accused as guilty?

- [ICCPR](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html), Article 14\(2\)

### Protection against compelled self-incrimination — 1

Standard: No compulsion to testify against oneself or confess guilt; exclude prohibited coercion and examine allegations of involuntary statements.

Stage: Obtaining/use of statements in a criminal process, including earlier investigation where relevant to trial.

Exceptions/applicability: Voluntary admission is not automatically prohibited. Distinguish Article 7 coercion and Article 9 detention guarantees; evaluate burden and exclusion of alleged coerced statements.

Observable components: Compulsion/coercion evidence; Voluntariness safeguards; Use of statements; Investigation/exclusion of coercion

Factual questions: How was the statement obtained and was compulsion alleged?; How did the court handle coercion allegations and use/exclusion?

- [ICCPR](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html), Article 14\(3\)\(g\)
- [HRC General Comment 32](https://cambodia.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Softlaw/GC%2032-A5-En.pdf), paragraph 41

Jurisdiction provisions to assess: [1987 Philippine Constitution](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/3/353), Article III sections 4 and 14; applicability must be assessed in the case; [Philippine Rules of Criminal Procedure](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/11/369), Rules 110 sections 6/8; 115 section 1; 116 sections 1/6; determine procedural applicability

Applicability: Historical prosecution, custodial statement and trial in 1995, followed by Supreme Court automatic review decided 1999\-02\-01\. ICCPR and the 1987 Constitution apply to those events; GC13 \(1984\) is contemporaneous guidance\. GC32 \(2007\) is used only as a later interpretive aid\. ICCPR 14\(3\)\(g\), related Article 7; Constitution Article III sections 12\(1\)\-\(3\),17; GC13 paragraphs 14\-15\. Later GC32 paragraph 41 is explicitly later interpretation; Article 7 concern is recorded separately and not an extra score\.

Provisional analyst explanation: The accused alleged a police threat against his life and a confession signed under pressure; the trial reasoning reproduced by the Court stressed lack of medical proof\. A threat can be psychological compulsion without a medical injury, so absence of a certificate alone does not resolve the allegation\. This is a specific possible concern about voluntariness/exclusion and the adequacy of investigation, not a finding that police torture occurred\. Contrary evidence is strong: the Court found the confession voluntary and accepted counsel's testimony about warnings, presence and an explained waiver\. The Court's general exclusion guideline is a safeguard it states, not independent proof of this accused's treatment\. Later GC32 paragraph 41 clarifies a State voluntariness burden; it is not presented as a rule newly issued in 1995 or as evidence the contemporaneous court applied it\.

Evidence confidence: moderate · Completeness: partial

Missing facts: Conditions before counsel arrived and investigation of the alleged threat; Original confession, waiver and full voluntariness testimony; Whether medical access was available and why no certificate was produced; The applicable contemporaneous admissibility/burden ruling and whether independent evidence depended on confession

Next verification: Examine the original voluntariness dispute and the State's safeguard evidence under ICCPR 14\(3\)\(g\), Article 7 and Constitution Article III sections 12/17; distinguish lack of corroboration from positive proof no coercion occurred\.
