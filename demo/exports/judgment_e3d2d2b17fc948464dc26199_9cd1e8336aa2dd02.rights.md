## Fair-trial dimensions

### Competent, independent, impartial tribunal established by law — insufficient_evidence

Standard: The adjudicating tribunal must be competent, independent, impartial and established by law.

Stage: Determination of a criminal charge or rights/obligations in a suit at law.

Exceptions/applicability: An adverse ruling alone does not establish bias. Assess special/military proceedings and law establishing jurisdiction; core independence/impartiality cannot be displaced by convenience.

Observable components: Lawful establishment and jurisdiction; Competence; Institutional independence; Actual/objective impartiality; Challenges and rulings

Factual questions: What law establishes the tribunal's authority?; What evidence addresses competence, interference, conflicts or objective appearance of bias?

- [ICCPR](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html), Article 14\(1\)
- [HRC General Comment 32](https://cambodia.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Softlaw/GC%2032-A5-En.pdf), paragraphs 18–22

Jurisdiction provisions to assess: [1987 Philippine Constitution](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/3/353), Article III sections 4 and 14; applicability must be assessed in the case; [Philippine Rules of Criminal Procedure](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/11/369), Rules 110 sections 6/8; 115 section 1; 116 sections 1/6; determine procedural applicability

Applicability: Criminal\-charge and judicial warrant\-review context, 1993\-1994; prosecutorial investigation and judicial adjudication are distinct\.

Provisional analyst explanation: The Court reports reliance on the prosecutor certification instead of personal evidence evaluation, and PACC involvement in the preliminary inquiry\. That documented warrant failure is separately retained under Article 9 and Constitution III\.2\. It does not by itself establish unlawful tribunal establishment, jurisdictional incompetence, institutional dependence or personal bias\. The replacement prosecutor panel and effective Supreme Court intervention qualify the institutional account\. An adverse or legally erroneous decision alone is not a tribunal\-independence finding\.

Evidence confidence: moderate · Completeness: limited

Missing facts: RTC establishment/jurisdiction law and judicial appointment/conflict facts not inspected\.; Original warrant order and any judicial recusal record absent\.

Next verification: Inspect a specific tribunal establishment, independence or impartiality proposition before assigning a score; do not transfer prosecutor affiliation to the judge\.

### Equality before courts and equality of arms — 2

Standard: Equality before courts and tribunals requires equal access and procedural opportunities; distinctions require objective, reasonable justification without actual disadvantage or unfairness.

Stage: Court/tribunal access and relevant proceedings, including criminal charges and suits at law.

Exceptions/applicability: Different treatment is not automatically unlawful; examine objective reasons, actual disadvantage and access barriers. Do not equate equal arms with identical roles.

Observable components: Access without discriminatory barriers; Comparable opportunity to present case; Material procedural disadvantage; Objective justification

Factual questions: Could the parties access the tribunal and make submissions?; Did disclosure, resources or procedure put one party at material disadvantage, with what justification?

- [ICCPR](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html), Article 14\(1\)
- [HRC General Comment 32](https://cambodia.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Softlaw/GC%2032-A5-En.pdf), paragraphs 7–14

Jurisdiction provisions to assess: [1987 Philippine Constitution](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/3/353), Article III sections 4 and 14; applicability must be assessed in the case; [Philippine Rules of Criminal Procedure](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/11/369), Rules 110 sections 6/8; 115 section 1; 116 sections 1/6; determine procedural applicability

Applicability: Article 14\(1\) fairness/equality in the described criminal\-charge process; the clearest direct finding concerns preliminary\-investigation material used for the later warrant\.

Provisional analyst explanation: Anchor 2: substantial documented procedural disadvantage\. Requested documents were reportedly not supplied despite an order; the Court expressly states that Bato counter\-affidavit was considered without an opportunity for petitioners to refute it\. That late material supported the information and ensuing warrant\. Some affidavits were disclosed, the panel was replaced, a warrant opposition was allowed and later judicial relief succeeded\. Those qualifications do not establish a contemporaneous opportunity to answer the late material before the challenged decision\. This provisional analysis overlaps disclosure facts in preparation\_facilities and must not be summed with them\.

Evidence confidence: high · Completeness: partial

Missing facts: Original affidavits, disclosure inventory, panel orders and hearing transcripts absent\.; Any lawful justification or earlier opportunity to answer Bato not fully documented beyond the judgment\.

Next verification: A qualified reviewer should confirm the late\-evidence chronology and how the disadvantage persisted into the post\-charge judicial decision\.

### Presumption of innocence — insufficient_evidence
