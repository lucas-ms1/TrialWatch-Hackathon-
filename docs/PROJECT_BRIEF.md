# Track 3: an interface connecting collective-action signals and advocacy strategy

October 9, 2026. The intended product is an **interface for advocacy strategy and early awareness of collective action**. It connects possible clusters of related cases with public news, social-media and civil-society reporting, then helps advocates investigate responses, find useful analogues, compare approaches and estimate likely advocacy outcomes when evidence supports doing so. Start with the Philippines; transfer to other jurisdictions must expose differences that affect the comparison.

The user's example is several reports of protest-related arrests in a similar area: propose a cluster for review, inspect public NGO/civil-society, news and social-media accounts for corroboration or challenge, distinguish emerging concern from collective action, and connect the evidence to options supporting affected cases and community agency. The aim includes helping advocates judge when increasing an issue's visibility may be useful. Neither a cluster nor publicity by itself establishes a movement or benefit to a case.

This direction extends the earlier pattern-review and campaign-comparison work. The [brief before this interface clarification](history/PROJECT_BRIEF.before-interface-2026-10-09.md), [previous brief](history/PROJECT_BRIEF.before-strategy-2026-10-09.md) and [October 7–8 brief](history/PROJECT_BRIEF.2026-10-08.md) remain historical records. An interface is the confirmed product format, with linked evidence, timelines, comparisons and editable briefs as possible views/exports. A named intended practitioner, exact interface priorities, outcome/horizon and owners remain open.

## One connected workflow

1. **Signals.** Inspect attributed reports with event, publication and retrieval dates. Future source connections could cover permitted public news, social-media and organizational material; no operational collection or monitoring has started.
2. **Cluster review.** Suggest related cases/events using actions, geography, time window, procedural stage and contextual similarities. Show each inclusion reason, exclusions, duplicate/report lineage and uncertain identities. A reviewer decides whether the candidate merits investigation. Cases, events and reports have separate counts; a handpicked demonstration rule is not a calibrated alert threshold.
3. **Public responses and collective action.** Inspect public NGO/civil-society reports and organization/event-level activity for support, contradiction and context. Label announced plans, source-reported activity underway/completed, and forecasts separately. A repost may repeat the same upstream claim; NGO reporting is not automatically independent corroboration.
4. **Analogues and strategy.** Compare relevant histories and approaches, investigate actors and engagement moments, and develop conditional outcome scenarios or estimates with transfer limits and uncertainty. Make issue visibility, case support and community priorities explicit objectives rather than presumed impacts.
5. **Human decision and outcome tracking.** Let an advocate select the next verification or engagement step, record the rationale and track later evidence against the stated objective. Preserve revisions so corrected evidence can change a strategy judgment visibly. Proposed action, completed engagement and observed outcome remain distinct.

The interface should distinguish three questions: **Is there an emerging pattern of reported arrests or other case concerns? Is publicly documented collective action planned or underway? What future mobilization or advocacy outcome is forecast?** Evidence for one does not automatically answer the others. More coverage does not establish more incidents. Collective action may be documented without a case cluster, and a cluster may have no documented collective response. The intended scope supports rights, cases and communities through public organization/event information; it does not track private individuals.

## What an advocate should be able to investigate

1. **Find analogues.** Retrieve cases or movements with relevant similarities in objectives, affected groups, institutions, legal setting, procedural stage, available tactics and constraints. Show the material differences and uncertain identity links. A similar history offers a comparison, not an interchangeable proxy for another population or jurisdiction.
2. **Compare advocacy approaches.** Connect each documented tactic to its objective, actors, target institution, timing, context and reported outcomes. Preserve unsuccessful approaches, unknown outcomes, contradictory accounts and source dependence. The intended question is which approaches appear most effective in which contexts; the current evidence does not answer it.
3. **Investigate actors and moments.** Trace who engaged whom and when, alongside procedural developments and public responses. Assess evidence for a contribution and competing explanations before treating an actor or moment as having the greatest impact. An event occurring after engagement establishes sequence, not causation.
4. **Estimate likely outcomes.** Use suitable comparable histories to make a bounded, revisable estimate about a defined future outcome. Show assumptions, evidence gaps, transfer limits and uncertainty; return “insufficient evidence” when an estimate is unsupported.
5. **Identify collective action and emerging concerns.** Connect reviewed case clusters with public responses while separately labeling announced/planned action, evidence of activity underway/completed, and forecasts of future action. A public announcement can identify activity before it occurs without being a prediction. An emerging arrest concern is a different target from movement activity. No live monitoring or collection has been authorized or started.

Evidence tables, timelines and cited editable briefs within the interface should help an advocate compare options, challenge a conclusion and choose the next evidence check. Counts of observations or sources do not measure reach, incidence, effectiveness or impact.

## Requirements for a credible outcome estimate

Each proposed estimate must declare the outcome, time window, starting situation and advocacy approach under consideration; the comparison histories and why they transfer; the evidence cutoff; assumptions and alternative explanations; and uncertainty. Legal developments, policy changes, public attention and movement activity are distinct outcomes. Source-reported developments must remain distinct from analyst judgments about their causes.

Describing outcomes after an approach, estimating what might happen next and estimating an approach's causal contribution are different claims. Effectiveness comparisons must address differences such as case selection, institutional context, concurrent legal work, other campaigns, resources, documentation coverage and outcome windows. Unknown or unreported outcomes cannot be coded as failures or successes.

Before presenting numerical likelihoods as reliable, define and evaluate a forecasting method on later outcomes withheld from the method, compare it with a simple baseline and inspect calibration and errors. Until evidence supports an estimate, the product should explain what is missing. The current nine-observation sample cannot justify percentages, a ranking of tactics or a claim about greatest impact. These are intended capabilities, not validated model outputs.

## What exists today

The local Python/SQLite application is **manual and deterministic**. Analysts can inspect attributed passages, make corrections, save reviews, classify observations, compare several campaign timelines and export editable Markdown/JSON. Saved analyses retain evidence snapshots; later evidence changes flag them for re-review. Duplicate links and unknowns remain visible.

The [pattern worksheet](PATTERN_REVIEW_WORKSHEET.md) and [proposal prompt](PATTERN_PROPOSAL_PROMPT.md) support preparation outside the app. An illustrative interface does not add source retrieval, cluster detection, automatic analogy retrieval, effectiveness estimation, actor-impact attribution, forecasting or collective-action detection to the application. Those capabilities remain proposed. Worksheets need manual evidence rechecking. The default application demonstration uses three fictional dossiers; local historical evidence and runtime state remain excluded from the synthetic public package.
