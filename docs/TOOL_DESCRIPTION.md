# TrialWatch Philippines: collective-action signals and advocacy strategy

The intended product is an interface connecting emerging case concerns and publicly documented collective action to advocacy strategy. It would help advocates review possible clusters, inspect relevant public news, social-media and NGO/civil-society accounts, find useful analogues across cases and movements, compare advocacy approaches across contexts and jurisdictions, investigate influential actors and moments, and estimate likely advocacy outcomes when evidence supports an estimate. The initial focus is the Philippines. An interface is confirmed; the named practitioner, exact priorities and owners remain open.

The connected workflow is **signals → cluster review → public responses and collective action → analogues and strategy → human decision and outcome tracking**. For example, several reports of protest-related arrests in one area could prompt review of related cases/events and public responses, then a comparison of options for supporting cases, community agency and useful issue visibility. Proposed clusters must show their action, time, geography and procedural-stage basis, identity uncertainty and contrary evidence. Reports, cases and events are different units. A cluster is not proof of a movement; more coverage is not proof of more incidents; an NGO source or repost is not automatically independent corroboration.

Comparisons should connect tactics, objectives, actors, target institutions, timing, context and reported outcomes, with excerpts, contrary evidence, unknowns and alternative explanations. Similar cases are not interchangeable proxies. An outcome estimate must specify the outcome, time window, context, evidence cutoff, assumptions and uncertainty, and can return “insufficient evidence.” Sequence alone does not establish effectiveness or causal impact. Emerging case concerns, publicly documented collective action and forecasts of future mobilization are distinct warning targets. Announced plans, source-reported activity and forecasts carry separate labels; a plan does not prove an event occurred. Scope is public organization/event information supporting rights and communities, not private individual tracking.

The accepted first example asks what records say about notice of charges and opportunities to respond in proceedings involving human-rights defenders, and what legal or public responses followed. Its local sample has nine AI-prepared, human-review-pending observations across three provisional histories from four public PDFs. It demonstrates evidence inspection, not effectiveness measurement or validated prediction.

The implemented baseline is a local, manual and deterministic campaign-comparison app. Analysts review attributed observations, preserve corrections, classify evidence, compare timelines and export editable Markdown/JSON. Saved analyses retain evidence snapshots and expose later changes. Automatic source retrieval, clustering, matching, effectiveness estimates, forecasting and collective-action detection are not implemented; external worksheets, prompts and any synthetic interface illustration are preparation only. The fictional protest-arrest illustration is separate from the real notice/response evidence drill.

Three fictional dossiers demonstrate existing application behavior. Historical research and runtime state remain separate from the synthetic public package. Practitioner usefulness, forecasting reliability and real-world impact remain unmeasured. Future source connections require permitted access and a defined collection scope; none has started. No live monitoring, outreach or automated collection runs. Original code/docs and synthetic fixtures are MIT licensed; third-party source rights remain separate.
