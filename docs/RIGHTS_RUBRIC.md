# Provisional rights rubric v2

Version `rights-rubric/2.0`, contract `rights-assessment/2`, October 9, 2026. This original hackathon framework exposes all thirteen requested core fair-trial dimensions, three conditional safeguards and the four preserved expression dimensions. It is not an official TrialWatch assessment, complete guarantee of legal compliance or calibrated probability.

The [v1 rubric](history/RIGHTS_RUBRIC.before-connected-intervention-2026-10-09.md) and immutable `reviewdesk/rights-rubric.v1.json` preserve the original nine definitions. V1 saved assessments, exports and reviews use those definitions. A new v2 draft requires an explicit new assessment and fresh review. Split counsel/preparation and changed tribunal/publicity dimensions never inherit a combined v1 score; newly introduced dimensions start not assessed. Due process is the umbrella, not an additional score or average.

## Concern anchors, defined before implementation

| Value | Meaning | Evidence and reasoning requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Documented safeguard; no specific concern on the assessed dimension | Affirmative relevant evidence and an explanation of how the safeguard addresses the dimension. Missing documents never qualify. This does not certify the whole proceeding. |
| 1 | Specific possible concern | A sourced factual proposition raises a relevant question, with material factual or legal explanation unresolved. |
| 2 | Substantial documented concern | The proposition and explained application indicate a substantial departure from the standard; contrary accounts and unresolved justifications remain visible. |
| 3 | Severe documented concern | Explain the gravity, duration or central importance of the documented departure. This remains provisional analysis, without an automatic legal finding. |

Scores describe documented concern on individual dimensions. They do not count articles, predict guilt, rank countries, measure advocacy impact or estimate a probability of violation. Reviewers may disagree on gravity; the chosen rule and rationale must be inspectable. Repeated reporting cannot raise a level or evidence confidence automatically. A contrary account does not automatically subtract a point: explain its consequences.

Non-numeric states: `not_assessed` means no assessment undertaken; `insufficient_evidence` means attempted assessment cannot support a numerical conclusion; `not_applicable` requires an applicability explanation. Each has a null score. None is zero. Every numeric dimension requires cited support and an explanation; gaps and next checks remain explicit.

Evidence confidence is separate: **low** (support or identity/source reliability substantially unresolved), **moderate** (support checked but material uncertainty remains), **high** (strong passage-to-proposition support with important competing accounts examined). These are evidence judgments, not model self-confidence. Completeness is **unknown** (not judged), **limited** (important material absent), **partial** or **substantial**, referring to the dimension's needed facts and documents, never the number of URLs. “Substantial” is not a claim that the complete court file was obtained. Assessments preserve the reasons for these choices.


## Framework, observable components and applicability

Primary sources: [ICCPR Articles 14 and 19](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html), [HRC General Comment 32](https://cambodia.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Softlaw/GC%2032-A5-En.pdf), and [General Comment 34](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/english/bodies/hrc/docs/GC34.pdf). These sources were inspected in the preceding bounded work; the expanded paragraph references were rechecked against the retained primary text in this continuation. No new case collection occurred.

The operational questions below are an analyst worksheet. A filled answer must cite exact support/contrary propositions and explain the fact-to-standard connection, uncertainty, concern, confidence/completeness, gaps and next check. Stage and jurisdiction must be assessed; no stage is inferred merely from a source title.

### Thirteen core fair-trial dimensions

| Dimension | Standard locator | Observable questions/components | Stage and exceptions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tribunal_law` — Competent, independent, impartial tribunal established by law | ICCPR Article 14(1); HRC General Comment 32 paragraphs 18–22 | What law establishes the tribunal's authority? What evidence addresses competence, interference, conflicts or objective appearance of bias? Components: Lawful establishment and jurisdiction, Competence, Institutional independence, Actual/objective impartiality, Challenges and rulings | Determination of a criminal charge or rights/obligations in a suit at law. An adverse ruling alone does not establish bias. Assess special/military proceedings and law establishing jurisdiction; core independence/impartiality cannot be displaced by convenience. |
| `equality_arms` — Equality before courts and equality of arms | ICCPR Article 14(1); HRC General Comment 32 paragraphs 7–14 | Could the parties access the tribunal and make submissions? Did disclosure, resources or procedure put one party at material disadvantage, with what justification? Components: Access without discriminatory barriers, Comparable opportunity to present case, Material procedural disadvantage, Objective justification | Court/tribunal access and relevant proceedings, including criminal charges and suits at law. Different treatment is not automatically unlawful; examine objective reasons, actual disadvantage and access barriers. Do not equate equal arms with identical roles. |
| `publicity` — Public hearings and public judgments | ICCPR Article 14(1); HRC General Comment 32 paragraphs 28–29 | Was the hearing open to the public, and were any exclusions justified? Was the judgment made public, subject to applicable exceptions? Components: Public hearing access, Recorded exclusion ground and scope, Public judgment, Exception justification; missing web copies are evidence gaps, not proof | Hearing and judgment in determination of a criminal charge or suit at law; determine hearing type, as not every pretrial/appeal step requires a public hearing. Press/public may be excluded for morals, public order, national security in a democratic society, private lives, or strictly necessary special circumstances where publicity prejudices justice. Judgment exceptions include juvenile interests or matrimonial/child guardianship disputes. |
| `presumption_innocence` — Presumption of innocence | ICCPR Article 14(2); HRC General Comment 32 paragraph 30 | Who bore the burden and what proof standard was used? Did authorities prejudge guilt or present the accused as guilty? Components: Burden and proof standard, Benefit of doubt, Official prejudgment, Presentation of accused | Criminal charge through adjudication. Prosecution, detention or an adverse verdict alone does not establish failure; distinguish official statements and presentation from a properly reached finding. |
| `notice_charges` — Notice of charges | ICCPR Article 14(3)(a); HRC General Comment 32 paragraph 31 | When did the person learn the charge and alleged acts? What details and language were provided? Components: Prompt timing, Nature/legal basis, Cause/alleged facts, Understandable language | Criminal charge, rather than only preliminary investigation before charges. Arrest reasons under Article 9 are a distinct related guarantee; formal notice must address law and alleged general facts, orally or in writing as appropriate. |
| `preparation_facilities` — Adequate time and facilities for defence | ICCPR Article 14(3)(b); HRC General Comment 32 paragraphs 32–33 | What preparation time, case complexity and disclosure were available? Were necessary adjournments/access requested and how were requests decided? Components: Preparation time, Access to relevant/incriminating/exculpatory material, Facilities, Adjournment requests and rulings | Preparation for a criminal charge, hearings and relevant defence steps. Adequacy depends on complexity, access and circumstances. Requests for additional time and reasoned rulings matter; document volume alone is not adequacy. |
| `legal_assistance` — Access to effective legal assistance | ICCPR Article 14(3)(b), (d); HRC General Comment 32 paragraphs 34, 37–38 | Was counsel chosen/assigned, accessible and confidential? Were required assistance and effective representation available, with what restrictions? Components: Choice/information about counsel, Access and confidentiality, Assignment/means/interests of justice, Effective assistance, Self-representation and justified restrictions | Criminal defence; distinguish custodial/investigation guarantees under Article 9 and applicable domestic law. Assigned assistance depends on interests of justice and lack of means for free assistance. Self-representation restrictions require objective sufficiently serious reasons; attributed counsel errors require case-specific responsibility analysis. |
| `participation` — Presence and meaningful participation | ICCPR Article 14(3)(d); HRC General Comment 32 paragraphs 36–37 | Was the accused present, informed and able to follow/respond? If absent or restricted, what notice/opportunity and reasons existed? Components: Presence, Notice/opportunity, Understanding and ability to participate, Reasoned absence/restriction justification | Criminal trial and defence participation. Proceedings in absence can be permissible in specific circumstances after sufficient notice and opportunity; verify steps informing the accused and justification, not absence alone. |
| `challenge_evidence` — Examine witnesses and present defence evidence | ICCPR Article 14(3)(e); HRC General Comment 32 paragraph 39 | Could material prosecution witnesses/evidence be challenged? Could relevant defence witnesses/evidence be presented under comparable conditions? Components: Prosecution witness examination, Defence witness presentation, Relevant evidence access, Requests, refusals and justification | Criminal trial and material defence evidence requests. This is not an unlimited right to call every witness. Identify relevance, requests, refusals, reasons and disadvantage. |
| `interpretation` — Interpretation where required | ICCPR Article 14(3)(f); HRC General Comment 32 paragraph 40 | What language ability and proceedings language are documented? Was free, adequate interpretation provided throughout relevant proceedings? Components: Demonstrated language need, Free interpreter, Adequacy/coverage, Requests and response | Criminal proceedings when courtroom language cannot be understood/spoken. Nationality or multilingual setting alone does not prove need or adequacy. This right does not require proceedings in the preferred language where the court language is sufficiently understood. |
| `self_incrimination` — Protection against compelled self-incrimination | ICCPR Article 14(3)(g); HRC General Comment 32 paragraph 41 | How was the statement obtained and was compulsion alleged? How did the court handle coercion allegations and use/exclusion? Components: Compulsion/coercion evidence, Voluntariness safeguards, Use of statements, Investigation/exclusion of coercion | Obtaining/use of statements in a criminal process, including earlier investigation where relevant to trial. Voluntary admission is not automatically prohibited. Distinguish Article 7 coercion and Article 9 detention guarantees; evaluate burden and exclusion of alleged coerced statements. |
| `undue_delay` — Trial without undue delay | ICCPR Article 14(3)(c); HRC General Comment 32 paragraph 35 | What intervals, procedural steps and reasons for delay are documented? How do complexity, party conduct, court conduct and detention affect adequacy? Components: Charge-to-final chronology, Complexity, Party/court conduct, Reasons and detention urgency | Criminal charge through final adjudication/appeal. Assess complexity, accused conduct and administrative/judicial handling. Elapsed time or a procedural deadline alone does not establish undue delay; custody increases need for expedition. |
| `appeal_review` — Review of conviction and sentence | ICCPR Article 14(5); HRC General Comment 32 paragraphs 45–51 | Was higher review available and effective for both conviction and sentence? What facts/law, judgment and record were reviewed and what barriers existed? Components: Higher tribunal access, Conviction and sentence coverage, Substantive review, Reasons/record access, Timing and barriers | After criminal conviction/sentence and relevant higher review. Guarantee is substantive, not merely a formal check. Civil review is not automatically within Article 14(5); examine applicable special procedures and access to reasoned judgment/record. |

### Conditional safeguards

| Dimension | Standard locator | Observable questions/components | Stage and exceptions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `juvenile_safeguards` — Conditional juvenile safeguards | ICCPR Article 14(4); HRC General Comment 32 paragraphs 42–44 | What age/status applies at the event date? What age-appropriate procedure, assistance and rehabilitation/diversion protections were used? Components: Age/status, Age-appropriate participation, Assistance/procedure, Rehabilitation and appropriate diversion | Criminal proceedings involving a juvenile at the relevant event date. Age and domestic juvenile status require verification. Juvenile-specific procedure/rehabilitation and diversion where appropriate are distinct from assuming identical adult procedure. |
| `double_jeopardy` — Conditional protection from double jeopardy | ICCPR Article 14(7); HRC General Comment 32 paragraphs 54–57 | Was a prior decision final, for the same offence in the same country? Is this a new prosecution or permitted review/resumption, on what grounds? Components: Prior final decision, Same offence/jurisdiction, New prosecution/punishment, Review/resumption distinction | Later criminal proceedings following a potentially final conviction/acquittal for the same offence. Verify finality/same offence/same jurisdiction. Higher review and narrowly justified resumption on exceptional grounds are distinct; the Covenant does not automatically prohibit prosecutions in different national jurisdictions. |
| `miscarriage_compensation` — Conditional miscarriage-of-justice compensation | ICCPR Article 14(6); HRC General Comment 32 paragraphs 52–53 | Do final conviction, punishment, qualifying reversal/pardon and new fact meet the conditions? What compensation route and non-disclosure exception apply? Components: Final conviction and punishment, Qualifying reversal/pardon, New conclusive fact, Non-disclosure responsibility, Compensation procedure | After qualifying reversal/pardon and punishment following final criminal conviction. Ordinary acquittal, dismissal or reversal alone is insufficient. Examine final conviction, punishment, newly discovered fact, conclusive miscarriage and whether non-disclosure was wholly/partly attributable to the person. |

### Preserved expression framework

| Dimension | Standard locator | Observable questions/components | Stage and exceptions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `expression_scope` — Expression and restrictive interference | ICCPR Article 19(1)–(2); HRC General Comment 34 paragraphs 5, 9–13 | What expression, restriction, law, asserted aim and particular justification are documented? What competing account or missing facts would change this dimension's assessment? Components: Specific expression/restriction nexus, Dimension-specific facts and competing justification | Identified expression and interference/restriction, including prosecution where linked. Do not presume expression was involved in every prosecution. Identify conduct and restriction; Article 20 duties and other rights may also require analysis. |
| `expression_legal_basis` — Restriction's legal basis | ICCPR Article 19(3); HRC General Comment 34 paragraphs 24–25 | What expression, restriction, law, asserted aim and particular justification are documented? What competing account or missing facts would change this dimension's assessment? Components: Specific expression/restriction nexus, Dimension-specific facts and competing justification | Identified expression and interference/restriction, including prosecution where linked. First establish expression and restriction. Citation of a statute alone does not settle foreseeability or its application. |
| `expression_legitimate_aim` — Asserted legitimate aim | ICCPR Article 19(3)(a)–(b); HRC General Comment 34 paragraphs 21–23, 28–32 | What expression, restriction, law, asserted aim and particular justification are documented? What competing account or missing facts would change this dimension's assessment? Components: Specific expression/restriction nexus, Dimension-specific facts and competing justification | Identified expression and interference/restriction, including prosecution where linked. An asserted aim is a government position, not proof of necessity or legitimacy in the particular application. Missing government reasons remain unknown. |
| `expression_necessity` — Necessity and proportionality | ICCPR Article 19(3); HRC General Comment 34 paragraphs 22, 33–35 | What expression, restriction, law, asserted aim and particular justification are documented? What competing account or missing facts would change this dimension's assessment? Components: Specific expression/restriction nexus, Dimension-specific facts and competing justification | Identified expression and interference/restriction, including prosecution where linked. Assess actual expression, aim, harm, penalty and alternatives. A restriction is not automatically unlawful; a legal basis and aim do not automatically establish necessity. |

Publicity evaluates actual access to hearings/judgments and lawful exclusions, including stage-specific limits for certain appeal/pretrial procedures. Reporting volume, online-document availability and news coverage are separate completeness observations. A missing public document proves neither compliance nor violation.

Related detention/arrest guarantees under Article 9 remain identifiable as distinct context: arrest reasons, prompt judicial control, trial within a reasonable time or release, and review of detention legality require their own standards and facts. They are not silently counted as Article 14 scores. Article 7 coercion can affect the self-incrimination assessment while retaining its separate source/standard.

Conditional safeguards need their triggering facts: juvenile age/procedure/rehabilitation; a prior final disposition and legally relevant same offence/jurisdiction for double jeopardy; or final conviction, suffered punishment and reversal/pardon grounded in a qualifying newly discovered fact for miscarriage compensation. Unknown triggering facts are not a default not-applicable conclusion. Ordinary nonfinal appeals, humanitarian pardons and retrial after quashing require separate analysis.

## Jurisdiction layer

For the Philippines, verify the [1987 Constitution](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/3/353), Article III §§4,12,14,16 and the [Revised Rules of Criminal Procedure](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/11/369), Rules 110 §§6,8; 115 §1; 116 §§1,6–8. Constitutional criminal-process and expression protections supplement the international framework. Investigative counsel and arraignment are distinct stages. Check amendments, applicable charge statutes and case-specific judicial interpretation before stronger domestic analysis; the cited compilation is not a guarantee that every procedural rule remains unmodified.

For Thailand, the government-hosted [Council of State English translation of the 2017 Constitution](https://www.ect.go.th/web-upload/1xff0d34e409a13ef56eea54c52a291126/202307/m_document/2046/13538/file_download/231044cc2539cec6ac4c1a0cb09d8a53.pdf) includes §§29 (presumption of innocence),34 (expression),188 (judicial independence/impartiality). Section 68's legal-aid policy sits within directive principles (§64); it is not silently treated as an identical individual guarantee. This government-hosted translation calls itself unofficial and identifies Thai as the authoritative legal text. Original language/version and criminal-procedure/charge provisions require a qualified check. Applicability to the event date matters.

An ICCPR profile is broader than any regional comparison. European, Inter-American and African judgments can be informative analogues; they are not binding domestic precedent for Philippine or Thai proceedings merely because an issue resembles them. Describe material institutional/legal differences and the authority's jurisdiction.

## Assessment and review contract

Each dimension retains applicable standard/citation, sourced propositions, support and contrary claim IDs, fact-to-standard rationale, anchored rule, score/state, separate confidence/completeness, missing facts and next verification step. Saved assessments retain date, rubric version, evidence revision/snapshot, author/preparation origin and review status. Human review records the exact dimensions and supporting/contrary observations inspected, reviewer, reason and time; approval is scoped, not whole-case authentication. Later corrections flag the saved snapshot stale and retain old text. Newly saved assessments require new review.

Sources retain allegations, government positions, reported court findings, published expert assessments and analyst judgments as different types. Wording, date roles, original language, translation status, identity uncertainty and upstream dependence remain visible. Schema and excerpt-presence checks establish reference integrity; they do not authenticate a document or prove its factual or legal support. Prepared AI material remains pending until a human reviews it. No fresh model runtime is integrated in this build: structured import and deterministic rules provide the runnable path.

## Relationship to TrialWatch and probability estimates

The public [TrialWatch grading annex](https://cfj.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/fair_trial_report_uganda_moses_bwayo.pdf), PDF p25, uses expert A/B/C/D/F overall grades with considerations including severity, effect on outcome, motives, harm and compatibility of law/procedure. Our per-dimension 0–3 scale does not implement or convert that methodology. Published grades belong in attributed external assessments, separately from prototype scores. The [public training site](https://trialwatchtraining.org/) describes standards and monitor training; signing into the course is required for registered learners. Full restricted modules were not inspected or represented as encoded.

There is no combined percentage or composite. Any later composite requires justified weights, coverage/abstention rules, sensitivity checks and a precise claim about what comparison means. Comparing profiles with different evidence coverage can mislead even if arithmetic is correct.

For defensible likelihood estimates, first define a legal question, jurisdiction, procedural stage, information cutoff and target label. Obtain independent expert labels with reasons, disagreement resolution and a documented sample frame; freeze outputs before labels are revealed. Separate cases and upstream sources across development and held-out tests, including temporal/jurisdictional testing and missingness. Calibrate on separate representative data, evaluate proper probability scores and calibration/uncertainty by subgroup and abstention coverage, and test with outside experts. Reading published conclusions then reproducing them is extraction with answer leakage, not independent evaluation. An ordinal concern level or model self-confidence is never a calibrated probability. These requirements are future work, with no legal-accuracy result claimed.
