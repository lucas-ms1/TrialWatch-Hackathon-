# Track 3: patterns across cases and advocacy responses

October 9, 2026. The latest direction broadens the project beyond comparing campaigns and outcomes. The [October 7–8 brief](PROJECT_BRIEF.2026-10-08.md) describes the earlier build, not a required first use case. No final question, dataset, example, audience, output format or owners have been agreed.

## Idea and practical value

Organize scattered trial information, case records, news and public responses into evidence a person can inspect across cases. Suggest connections through actions, affected groups, institutions, legal provisions, locations, procedural stages and responses. Some features can use agreed categories; others may emerge while reading and remain tentative until checked.

The Track 3 value is helping an advocate move from a possible issue to its supporting cases, contradictory evidence, documented advocacy actors and the next investigation question. A cited editable brief is one possible endpoint. An evidence table may make comparison easier; a timeline may make event order and gaps clearer. Choose by watching someone try a concrete advocacy task, not by assuming a memo or dashboard is required.

## Explore before selecting

1. Inspect the [material inventory](../MATERIALS_AND_DEMO_OPTIONS.md). Confirm access, permitted use, dates, languages and case/event identity. A packet link is not possession of a dataset or permission for model processing.
2. Try a few permitted passages across different histories and material types. Ask what repeated feature is observable, which reports share an upstream account and what contradicts the initial interpretation. Keep unsuccessful checks visible.
3. Pick one candidate question and one user task after that trial. Possibilities include deciding which pattern merits investigation, preparing a sourced briefing paragraph, or identifying documented actors and missing evidence before considering a response.
4. Test the output with a teammate or, if available, an intended user: can they locate support, find the strongest challenge, and choose a sensible next check? Label teammate feedback separately from practitioner feedback; neither establishes rights impact.

## Recommendation after the local material check

Start with **one possible recurring procedural feature and the documented advocacy responses around it**, using a small evidence slice. A proposed ceiling is 3–5 distinct case histories and 8–12 observations; fewer usable records or no credible pattern is an acceptable result. Do not force the previous pair or choose journalist arrests as the default. The actual feature and histories remain unselected.

The existing application supports reviewing several dossiers, carrying excerpts into comparisons and exposing corrections. The historical sample can help test record structure, but cannot establish a regional increase, independent corroboration, representative coverage or a forecast. If suitable real evidence is unavailable or permissions unresolved, a clearly fictional walkthrough can demonstrate the mechanism alongside an honest access/gap inventory. Existing fictional fixtures test the software; they do not select the team's substantive use case.

The smallest next improvement is a **candidate-pattern review worksheet across records**, with supporting/challenging evidence, shared-source warnings, uncertain case/event links, alternative explanations and a next-check decision. The [blank worksheet](../PATTERN_REVIEW_WORKSHEET.md) and [model proposal prompt](../PATTERN_PROPOSAL_PROMPT.md) are prepared. They are working documents, not an implemented discovery feature or a settled final format.

## What we can responsibly demonstrate

| Capability | Meaning | Status / evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Find a possible pattern | Several reviewed records may share a meaningful feature | Candidate for investigation; inspect support, contrary records, identities and source lineage |
| Detect a change in documented events | Comparable periods contain a change under a declared collection rule | Requires event deduplication, stable source/language coverage, windows and denominators; current sample does not establish this |
| Forecast a future development | Predict an outcome that has not happened | Longer-term ambition; needs time-separated evaluation, baselines, calibration and false-alert assessment; not demonstrated |

A flag is not a finding of abuse or illegality. Allegations, reported court findings, analyst interpretations and proposed advocacy remain distinct. Multiple reports may describe one event. A later legal development does not establish an advocacy effect; a single case outcome does not establish national impact.
