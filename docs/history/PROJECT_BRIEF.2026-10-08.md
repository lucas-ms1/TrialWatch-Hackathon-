# Track 3: Campaign Evidence Comparison

**User:** an advocacy analyst examining campaigns around reported proceedings in the Philippines. **Task:** compare documented social-media activity, international attention and campaign actions with reported legal/policy outcomes, while identifying evidence gaps and alternative explanations. **Output:** a cited comparison memo with source-linked timelines and editable analyst interpretation.

The user selected this direction after considering a briefing workspace. The existing evidence review/correction components remain useful, but campaign comparison now defines the product.

## Choose a feasible version of the challenge

| Option | What it would answer | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Descriptive campaign/outcome evidence comparison | What is documented, in what order, with what outcome evidence and limits? | Build now |
| Causal effectiveness ranking | Which advocacy strategy produced a better outcome? | Do not claim; requires systematic intervention/outcome/confounder data and credible design |
| Social-media monitoring dashboard | How attention changes across a sampled platform over time | Defer live collection; platform access and a sampling protocol are absent |
| Standalone advocacy briefing | What an analyst proposes doing next | Retain reusable backend; comparison memo is now primary |

## Smallest compelling workflow

Review selected statements with exact excerpts; preserve source and date roles. Correct with originals retained. Assign each selected observation a primary role: social media, international attention, campaign action, legal outcome, policy outcome or context. Save analyst interpretation, alternative explanations and limitations. Compare at least two dossiers and export a memo that includes both source evidence and unresolved questions.

The three synthetic dossiers deliberately contrast an attention/dismissal sequence, an activity/conviction sequence with incomplete attention evidence, and a consultation announcement with no known case outcome. They illustrate questions an analyst can ask, not measured relationships. No chart or count is labeled reach or impact.

## Fit to the official rubric

| Criterion | Points | What the demonstration substantiates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Innovation | 25 | An inspectable comparison joining campaign observations, legal/policy developments and alternative explanations |
| Feasibility | 25 | Complete local review/coding/comparison/export path, persistent revisions, no service dependency and offline fallback |

These are demonstration aims, not predicted scores or measured rights outcomes.

## What remains unproven

The workflow has not been validated by an advocacy practitioner. At mentoring, ask an intended user to compare two permitted dossiers, trace one outcome to its source, identify an alternative explanation and decide what evidence is missing. Record confusion and whether the memo would be used in their existing workflow. A subsequent systematic study would define campaign inclusion, source windows, coding and outcome measures before collection.

Current historical research was a triage convenience sample, not campaign evaluation. English-language reporting and publisher selection limit coverage. Source absence, social engagement, case outcome and national impact are different constructs. See [ANALYSIS_METHOD.md](../ANALYSIS_METHOD.md).
