# Rights assessment method and limits

The primary method is [RIGHTS_RUBRIC v1](RIGHTS_RUBRIC.md). Concern is an anchored ordinal judgment on a specific legal dimension, separate from evidence confidence and completeness. Missing evidence does not become a low score; repeated reporting does not automatically increase concern or confidence. Profiles have no combined percentage or calibrated probability.

Read the attributed proposition and exact supporting/contrary passages before choosing applicability and a rule. Identify competing accounts, legal exceptions, uncertain identity, event/publication/retrieval dates, source dependence and translation status. Explain why facts matter under the cited standard and what further verification is needed. Scope human approval to the dimensions and observations actually inspected.

Saved assessment versions retain evidence snapshots. Correcting evidence keeps the original and flags saved work stale; resaving and reviewing makes the change explicit. Preparation provenance, current review and published external expert assessment are different statuses.

Selected cases are a convenience sample, with reporting/access/language/selection bias. Case candidates, documents, defendants and arrest events are distinct. Counts and patterns describe the collected corpus under its filters, not national incidence, systemic misconduct, coordinated movements, reach or effect. Hypotheses need alternative explanations and further evidence. Country indicators provide separate year-specific context and cannot alter individual scores.

Independent expert labels, leakage prevention and calibration requirements are documented in the rubric. Published reports used to prepare examples cannot independently validate our assessments. Software checks measure persistence/validation behavior, not legal accuracy or advocacy impact. The preserved earlier descriptive campaign method is in `docs/history/ANALYSIS_METHOD.before-rights-pivot-2026-10-09.md`.
