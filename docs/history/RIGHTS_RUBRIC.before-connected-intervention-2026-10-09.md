# Provisional rights rubric v1

Version `rights-rubric/1.0`, October 9, 2026. This original hackathon rubric organizes evidence for advocacy prioritization and comparison. It is not an official TrialWatch assessment, expert grade, complete legal checklist or calibrated probability. Qualified human review remains necessary. Legal sources were inspected today; source captures/access receipts are local in `outputs/rights_pivot_20261009/legal/`.

## Concern anchors, defined before implementation

| Value | Meaning | Evidence and reasoning requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Documented safeguard; no specific concern on the assessed dimension | Affirmative relevant evidence and an explanation of how the safeguard addresses the dimension. Missing documents never qualify. This does not certify the whole proceeding. |
| 1 | Specific possible concern | A sourced factual proposition raises a relevant question, with material factual or legal explanation unresolved. |
| 2 | Substantial documented concern | The proposition and explained application indicate a substantial departure from the standard; contrary accounts and unresolved justifications remain visible. |
| 3 | Severe documented concern | Explain the gravity, duration or central importance of the documented departure. This remains provisional analysis, without an automatic legal finding. |

Scores describe documented concern on individual dimensions. They do not count articles, predict guilt, rank countries, measure advocacy impact or estimate a probability of violation. Reviewers may disagree on gravity; the chosen rule and rationale must be inspectable. Repeated reporting cannot raise a level or evidence confidence automatically. A contrary account does not automatically subtract a point: explain its consequences.

Non-numeric states: `not_assessed` means no assessment undertaken; `insufficient_evidence` means attempted assessment cannot support a numerical conclusion; `not_applicable` requires an applicability explanation. Each has a null score. None is zero. Every numeric dimension requires cited support and an explanation; gaps and next checks remain explicit.

Evidence confidence is separate: **low** (support or identity/source reliability substantially unresolved), **moderate** (support checked but material uncertainty remains), **high** (strong passage-to-proposition support with important competing accounts examined). These are evidence judgments, not model self-confidence. Completeness is **unknown** (not judged), **limited** (important material absent), **partial** or **substantial**, referring to the dimension's needed facts and documents, never the number of URLs. “Substantial” is not a claim that the complete court file was obtained. Assessments preserve the reasons for these choices.

## Dimensions and primary standards

The [ICCPR text](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html) establishes criminal-process guarantees in Article 14 and expression in Article 19. It distinguishes rights from permitted restrictions. The table references [Human Rights Committee General Comment 32](https://cambodia.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/Softlaw/GC%2032-A5-En.pdf) and [General Comment 34](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/english/bodies/hrc/docs/GC34.pdf); paragraph locators are stable even when PDF pagination differs.

| Dimension ID | Standard and locator | Facts to seek and questions to explain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `notice_charges` | ICCPR 14(3)(a); GC32 ¶31 | Charge, legal provision, alleged acts, notice time/language and procedural stage. Distinguish formal criminal charges from preliminary investigation and arrest reasons under Article 9(2). |
| `counsel_preparation` | ICCPR 14(3)(b),(d); GC32 ¶¶32–34,37–38 | Counsel access/privacy, preparation time, disclosure, requests and rulings; assignment where required, means and effective representation. Adequacy depends on circumstances. |
| `challenge_evidence` | ICCPR 14(3)(e); GC32 ¶39 | Material witness/evidence, defense request, allowed opportunity, restriction, justification and consequences. The right does not require every requested witness. |
| `independent_impartial` | ICCPR 14(1); GC32 ¶¶19–21 | Tribunal, interference/conflict, objective appearance of bias, challenge and reasoned ruling. An adverse verdict alone is not bias. |
| `presumption_innocence` | ICCPR 14(2); GC32 ¶30 | Burden/standard of proof, official prejudgment and treatment; detention or an allegation alone is not proof of guilt. |
| `expression_scope` | ICCPR 19(1),(2); GC34 ¶¶9–13 | The actual expression, speaker/medium and alleged restriction or prosecution link. Lack of the speech itself can prevent assessment. |
| `expression_legal_basis` | ICCPR 19(3); GC34 ¶¶22–27 | Identify the provision/version and application; accessibility, precision and discretion. A statute's existence alone does not settle compatibility. |
| `expression_legitimate_aim` | ICCPR 19(3)(a),(b); GC34 ¶¶28–32 | Attribute the asserted aim and connect it to an allowed ground; distinguish an assertion from its evidential justification. |
| `expression_necessity` | ICCPR 19(3); GC34 ¶¶33–35,47 | Specific threat, connection to expression, alternatives, sanction/harm and proportionality; examine government rationale and defamation safeguards where relevant. |

Applicability is assessed per dimension. Article 14(1)'s hearing protections extend to criminal charges and determination of rights/obligations in a suit at law; criminal minimum guarantees are not automatically a checklist for every administrative or pre-charge step. Public-hearing exceptions and proceedings in absence need their own facts. Other guarantees (delay, interpretation, self-incrimination, appeal, double jeopardy and juvenile protections) may require a future rubric version. Omission here never means those rights are irrelevant. Expression restrictions may meet legal requirements; arrest, prosecution, conviction or restriction does not itself establish unlawfulness. Article 20 and valid emergency derogations require separate expert examination; recording an emergency claim does not establish a lawful derogation. The prototype has no automated exception adjudicator.

## Jurisdiction layer

For the Philippines, verify the [1987 Constitution](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/3/353), Article III §§4,12,14,16 and the [Revised Rules of Criminal Procedure](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/11/369), Rules 110 §§6,8; 115 §1; 116 §§1,6–8. Constitutional criminal-process and expression protections supplement the international framework. Investigative counsel and arraignment are distinct stages. Check amendments, applicable charge statutes and case-specific judicial interpretation before stronger domestic analysis; the cited compilation is not a guarantee that every procedural rule remains unmodified.

For Thailand, the government-hosted [Council of State English translation of the 2017 Constitution](https://www.ect.go.th/web-upload/1xff0d34e409a13ef56eea54c52a291126/202307/m_document/2046/13538/file_download/231044cc2539cec6ac4c1a0cb09d8a53.pdf) includes §§29 (presumption of innocence),34 (expression),188 (judicial independence/impartiality). Section 68's legal-aid policy sits within directive principles (§64); it is not silently treated as an identical individual guarantee. This government-hosted translation calls itself unofficial and identifies Thai as the authoritative legal text. Original language/version and criminal-procedure/charge provisions require a qualified check. Applicability to the event date matters.

An ICCPR profile is broader than any regional comparison. European, Inter-American and African judgments can be informative analogues; they are not binding domestic precedent for Philippine or Thai proceedings merely because an issue resembles them. Describe material institutional/legal differences and the authority's jurisdiction.

## Assessment and review contract

Each dimension retains applicable standard/citation, sourced propositions, support and contrary claim IDs, fact-to-standard rationale, anchored rule, score/state, separate confidence/completeness, missing facts and next verification step. Saved assessments retain date, rubric version, evidence revision/snapshot, author/preparation origin and review status. Human review records the exact dimensions and supporting/contrary observations inspected, reviewer, reason and time; approval is scoped, not whole-case authentication. Later corrections flag the saved snapshot stale and retain old text. Newly saved assessments require new review.

Sources retain allegations, government positions, reported court findings, published expert assessments and analyst judgments as different types. Wording, date roles, original language, translation status, identity uncertainty and upstream dependence remain visible. Schema and excerpt-presence checks establish reference integrity; they do not authenticate a document or prove its factual or legal support. Prepared AI material remains pending until a human reviews it. No fresh model runtime is integrated in this build: structured import and deterministic rules provide the runnable path.

## Relationship to TrialWatch and probability estimates

The public [TrialWatch grading annex](https://cfj.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/fair_trial_report_uganda_moses_bwayo.pdf), PDF p25, uses expert A/B/C/D/F overall grades with considerations including severity, effect on outcome, motives, harm and compatibility of law/procedure. Our per-dimension 0–3 scale does not implement or convert that methodology. Published grades belong in attributed external assessments, separately from prototype scores. The [public training site](https://trialwatchtraining.org/) describes standards and monitor training; signing into the course is required for registered learners. Full restricted modules were not inspected or represented as encoded.

There is no combined percentage or composite. Any later composite requires justified weights, coverage/abstention rules, sensitivity checks and a precise claim about what comparison means. Comparing profiles with different evidence coverage can mislead even if arithmetic is correct.

For defensible likelihood estimates, first define a legal question, jurisdiction, procedural stage, information cutoff and target label. Obtain independent expert labels with reasons, disagreement resolution and a documented sample frame; freeze outputs before labels are revealed. Separate cases and upstream sources across development and held-out tests, including temporal/jurisdictional testing and missingness. Calibrate on separate representative data, evaluate proper probability scores and calibration/uncertainty by subgroup and abstention coverage, and test with outside experts. Reading published conclusions then reproducing them is extraction with answer leakage, not independent evaluation. An ordinal concern level or model self-confidence is never a calibrated probability. These requirements are future work, with no legal-accuracy result claimed.
