# Product brief: evidence-backed rights index

October 9 explicit user pivot. Track 3: Advocacy and Impact remains selected. The product turns evidence into usable advocacy prioritization and comparison through a searchable case index limited to fair-trial rights and freedom of expression. It overlaps Track 2 when linking facts to law; no organizer-approved change is claimed. This is an original hackathon prototype, not an official TrialWatch index.

The first user task is to inspect a case dimension, decide which concern can be responsibly carried forward and identify the next verification step. The practitioner and task owner remain unassigned. The notice/opportunity-to-respond evidence drill remains usable but does not constrain the broader rights dimensions. Advocacy strategy, movement detection and outcome forecasting are superseded as the main build.

The core flow is supplied document/structured evidence → attributed propositions and passages → applicable standard → provisional anchored dimension profile → named-human correction/review → searchable index → cited export. Supporting comparisons show why cases resemble one another, important differences, public reporting and context. Pattern suggestions remain investigative hypotheses with alternative explanations and evidence gaps.

The synthetic walkthrough implements manual structured intake and deterministic rule-based scoring. No fresh extraction model is integrated. Nine starting dimensions span charges/counsel/evidence/hearing/innocence and expression scope/legal basis/aim/necessity. They are an incomplete checklist and require applicability review. Separate fair-trial/expression profiles precede any justified future composite. See [rubric](RIGHTS_RUBRIC.md) and [technical scope](TECHNICAL_PLAN.md).

Use a small selected pilot of approximately 3–5 histories across Philippines/Thailand where available records support the workflow. Keep real captures/notes ignored and synthetic examples shareable. Country indicators provide year-specific context only. Third-party rights remain separate from MIT. The public-source inventory provides a workable route without any TrialWatch database promise or NDA dependency.

A good demonstration lets a person trace a score to a passage, see a competing account, leave a dimension unscored for missing evidence, approve only inspected dimensions, reopen saved work, detect stale assessment after correction and export the exact scope. Software tests verify those mechanics. Independent legal review and advocate usefulness testing are future work.

Earlier briefs are preserved in `docs/history/`; historical predictions, metrics and limited source-sequence reviews retain their original scope.
