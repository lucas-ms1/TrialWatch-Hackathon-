# Evidence method for candidate patterns and comparison

## Current strategy question and claim levels

The user now intends the product to find comparable cases and movements, compare advocacy approaches across contexts/jurisdictions, assess actors and engagement moments, and estimate likely advocacy outcomes. The accepted notice/opportunity-to-respond question remains the first worked example. Its nine observations are AI-prepared and human-review-pending, not an effectiveness study.

Use four explicit levels: **analogue** (why a history is relevant, and where it differs), **observed result** (what a source reports), **contribution assessment** (evidence connecting an action to a result, with competing explanations), and **forecast** (a future outcome, time horizon, information cutoff and uncertainty). Similarity does not establish transferability. Predicting an outcome among cases where a tactic was used does not establish how much choosing that tactic changes the outcome. Keep the forecasting target separate from any causal contrast.

Define the objective and outcome before comparing effectiveness. Record the unit, baseline conditions, jurisdiction, procedural stage, action and actor, recipient, timing, outcome window, competing actions, resources when known, missing results and source dependence. Keep unsuccessful and unresolved episodes visible. Show an unavailable estimate when the evidence does not support one; the present batch supports no success rate or probability.

For the secondary collective-action component, code **announced/planned**, **reported underway**, **completed**, **cancelled/postponed** or **unknown** only where sources support the status; any prediction is separately labeled with an as-of time. An announcement does not establish that an event occurred. Several defendants, filings, posts or reports do not alone establish coordinated collective action. No collection/monitoring is running.

## October 9 extension: cross-case candidate review

The following method extends the existing evidence discipline; it does not claim new automated analysis. The notice/response question is accepted for the first sample; the histories, specific practitioner and final output remain provisional. Use the [blank pattern worksheet](PATTERN_REVIEW_WORKSHEET.md) alongside a versioned evidence batch/export.

Record common features (actions, affected groups, institutions, legal provisions, places, procedural stages, public responses) and tentative emergent labels. Define each label and link every assignment to a narrow source-attributed claim and excerpt. Preserve original-language passages, translation status and event/publication/retrieval dates. Keep allegations, reported court findings, reported activities, analyst judgments and proposed advocacy distinct.

Adjudicate case and event links separately; a name match or shared dossier is insufficient. Record certain, possible and rejected links with reasons. Report counts, observation counts, unique events and legal cases are different units. The existing app counts selected observations and cited URLs, not verified unique cases/events.

For apparent corroboration, inspect whether the accounts rely on the same press release, witness, document, wire report or earlier article. Mark shared, separately evidenced or unknown lineage with a basis; different publishers/URLs do not establish independence. Retain contradictions even if most reports agree. More coverage is not more incidents.

A candidate pattern needs supporting records, counterevidence or explicit gaps, plausible alternative explanations, source/period/language coverage and the next human investigation step. Record relevant advocacy actors and what they reportedly proposed or did; do not infer available influence, successful contact or impact. Human disposition may be investigate, narrow, reject or needs-check. “No support found in this batch” is not proof of absence.

Separate finding a repeated feature from detecting change under comparable sampling and from forecasting. A convenience sample cannot establish an increase or predict escalation. For change detection, first define event units, deduplication, source coverage, comparable windows and denominators. Forecasting requires a separate time-held-out evaluation. The frozen historical AI experiment establishes neither.

External worksheet copies have no automated revision tracking. Record evidence batch and saved analysis/review revisions, then manually recheck every supporting/challenging dependency when evidence changes. The app's existing automatic stale warnings apply to its saved analyses only.

## Existing manual campaign-comparison method

The unit shown is a **coded source-attributed observation within a dossier**. A dossier is an analyst-selected group of records around a campaign/proceeding; it is not automatically one unique legal case. Multiple reports can repeat the same event. The public demonstration is wholly synthetic.

## Coding rules

### Operational definitions for the proposed research batch

The team has not yet adopted a pair, period or final protocol. Before collection, define a **campaign** as an identifiable coordinated effort with an actor/network, an explicit objective and a relationship to a specified proceeding. A solitary sympathetic article or statement can be an observation without proving that a coordinated campaign exists. Use an analyst label such as "documented advocacy around [proceeding]" where coordination is uncertain.

**International attention** is a documented statement, communication or action by an identifiable actor outside the Philippines or an international body. Record actor, recipient and action. An appeal for international attention is evidence of the appeal, not evidence that the requested attention occurred. A foreign publisher reporting a local event is not automatically an international advocacy action.

An **observation** is one narrow attributed claim supported by an excerpt. Give repeated reports about one event a shared event ID when the match is supported; preserve uncertain linkage. Dossier, person, proceeding, report and event counts are different. The app counts coded observations and exact cited URLs, and does not deduplicate or measure activity volume from event IDs.

A **legal development/outcome** identifies the proceeding, person, charge and stage: filing, detention, bail, dismissal, conviction, appeal, release order and actual release are distinct. Finality/current status require specific evidence. A **policy development** must name its stage: advocacy demand, government proposal, adoption, commencement or implementation. A campaign demand belongs in campaign action, not enacted policy. A draft or consultation does not establish national reform or effects.

For a small first batch, use the same declared source/language coverage and search-time cap for each history, record inclusion/exclusion reasons, and check every retained row with a second human. Missing activity means no qualifying record was found in that bounded search; it does not establish no activity. Equal observation-window length does not match legal merits, procedural stages, publicity or resources. Selection from familiar, English-language, accessible histories remains biased. See [RESEARCH_HANDOFF.md](RESEARCH_HANDOFF.md) for the record and exchange protocol.

| Primary category | Include | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social media | A documented public post, campaign hashtag or supplied platform observation | Audience size, reach, engagement or public opinion from a source count |
| International attention | A documented statement/report by an international actor | Influence, pressure or endorsement beyond the source |
| Campaign action | A reported advocacy action with attributed evidence | Completion, recipient response or impact not supported by the passage |
| Legal outcome | A source-reported court disposition or legal development | An authenticated court finding, finality or current case status |
| Policy development (stored as policy_outcome) | A documented policy announcement, decision or implementation step | Enactment from a draft or national impact from an announcement |
| Context | Other evidence relevant to interpreting the sequence | A causal explanation merely because it appears nearby |

The analyst chooses one primary category per selected statement. Real material with multiple meanings should be separated into narrow supported statements or discussed explicitly in interpretation. Coding is manual; automatic validation checks structure and text presence, not substantive accuracy. Correct quoted evidence only through the evidence review, preserving originals.

## Read the timeline honestly

Sort known event dates; keep unknown event dates in a separate group. Publication, retrieval/recording and event dates have different roles. A scheduled event is not a completed event. A reported court outcome stays attributed to its source. New corrections do not alter an existing analysis snapshot silently.

Category totals count coded observations; source totals count distinct cited URLs within the specified scope. Repeated coverage and shared upstream reporting can inflate both without new evidence. These are not measures of attention volume or independent corroboration. Missing category/outcome evidence is **unknown**, not zero activity or no change.

## Interpret without causal claims

For each dossier, record the analytical question, a bounded interpretation, alternative explanations and limitations. Consider legal arguments, normal procedure, other advocates, changes in government priorities, selection/publication bias and reverse causality: a court event can attract attention rather than result from it. Do not rank effectiveness from the example sequences or compare unlike outcomes as if cases had equivalent starting conditions.

A legal case outcome and country-level change are different scales. Draft consultation, adopted policy, implementation and demonstrated effects are different stages. This prototype records source evidence; it does not measure national impact, causal contribution or a counterfactual.

## A later real evidence study

Before collecting data, agree with a partner on campaign/proceeding definitions, inclusion criteria, source/language/platform coverage, time windows and outcomes. Record missingness, source access limits, linkage uncertainty and competing events. Use permitted primary material where available, document engagement metric definitions/denominators, and have a second human coder inspect a sample. Systematically gathered descriptive evidence would still require a credible identification strategy to support causal claims.

This research protocol has not run. The historical 20-report experiment is neither representative campaign evidence nor a human-ground-truth advocacy evaluation.
