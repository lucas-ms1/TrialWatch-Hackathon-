# What this comparison can establish

The unit shown is a **coded source-attributed observation within a dossier**. A dossier is an analyst-selected group of records around a campaign/proceeding; it is not automatically one unique legal case. Multiple reports can repeat the same event. The public demonstration is wholly synthetic.

## Coding rules

| Primary category | Include | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Social media | A documented public post, campaign hashtag or supplied platform observation | Audience size, reach, engagement or public opinion from a source count |
| International attention | A documented statement/report by an international actor | Influence, pressure or endorsement beyond the source |
| Campaign action | A reported advocacy action with attributed evidence | Completion, recipient response or impact not supported by the passage |
| Legal outcome | A source-reported court disposition or legal development | An authenticated court finding, finality or current case status |
| Policy development (stored as policy_outcome) | A documented policy announcement, decision or implementation step | Enactment from a draft or national impact from an announcement |
| Context | Other evidence relevant to interpreting the sequence | A causal explanation merely because it appears nearby |

The analyst chooses one primary category per selected statement. Real material with multiple meanings should be separated into narrow supported statements or discussed explicitly in interpretation. Coding is manual; automatic validation checks structure and text presence, not substantive accuracy. Correct quoted evidence only through the evidence review, preserving originals.

## Read the timeline honestly

Sort known event dates; keep unknown event dates in a separate group. Publication, retrieval/recording and event dates have different roles. A scheduled event is not a completed event. A reported court outcome stays attributed to its source. New corrections do not alter an existing analysis snapshot silently.

Category totals count coded observations; source totals count distinct cited URLs within the specified scope. Repeated coverage and shared upstream reporting can inflate both without new evidence. These are not measures of attention volume or independent corroboration. Missing category/outcome evidence is **unknown**, not zero activity or no change.

## Interpret without causal claims

For each dossier, record the analytical question, a bounded interpretation, alternative explanations and limitations. Consider legal arguments, normal procedure, other advocates, changes in government priorities, selection/publication bias and reverse causality: a court event can attract attention rather than result from it. Do not rank effectiveness from the example sequences or compare unlike outcomes as if cases had equivalent starting conditions.

A legal case outcome and country-level change are different scales. Draft consultation, adopted policy, implementation and demonstrated effects are different stages. This prototype records source evidence; it does not measure national impact, causal contribution or a counterfactual.

## A later real evidence study

Before collecting data, agree with a partner on campaign/proceeding definitions, inclusion criteria, source/language/platform coverage, time windows and outcomes. Record missingness, source access limits, linkage uncertainty and competing events. Use permitted primary material where available, document engagement metric definitions/denominators, and have a second human coder inspect a sample. Systematically gathered descriptive evidence would still require a credible identification strategy to support causal claims.

This research protocol has not run. The historical 20-report experiment is neither representative campaign evidence nor a human-ground-truth advocacy evaluation.
