### Earlier October 7 product choice, retained for context

On October 7 the user selected **comparison of campaigns and case outcomes**, specifically the packet p8 example connecting social media, international attention and human-rights campaigns with policy changes or legal-case outcomes. The table below records that build. It is superseded as a scope constraint by the October 9 direction, and never constituted an organizer requirement or validation result.

| Feasible direction | User task and output | Fit and evidence constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Campaign and case-outcome comparison — selected** | An analyst codes cited evidence of social-media activity, international attention, campaign actions, reported legal outcomes and policy developments, compares saved dossiers, and exports an editable comparison with gaps and alternative explanations | Directly follows the user's chosen packet example. Three synthetic dossiers demonstrate different attention records, two reported legal outcomes and a draft policy consultation whose case outcome remains unknown. A real comparison would require a purpose-built evidence set; no effectiveness ranking is claimed. |
| Evidence-to-advocacy briefing workspace | An analyst selects an objective, audience and next step, then exports a cited case briefing | Reuses evidence, correction and export components and can support later handoff, but does not by itself answer the selected cross-campaign comparison task. |
| Actor and intervention-moment map | An analyst maps relevant actors, possible engagement routes and dated opportunities | Feasible as a manual worksheet, but current influence, engagement access and urgency are not supported by the existing historical sample. A map alone provides a less complete advocacy handoff. |

The selected workflow helps an analyst compare **what campaign and attention evidence was recorded, which legal or policy outcomes were reported, what remains unknown, and which other explanations could account for the sequence**. Its output is a source-attributed comparison for human review. Counts describe selected coded statements in these dossiers, not social reach, engagement, public attention volume, prevalence or effectiveness. Missing outcome evidence means unknown, not no outcome. Chronology does not show that a campaign caused an outcome, and a legal-case outcome does not establish national policy impact. Real-world usefulness and advocacy effectiveness remain unmeasured.
